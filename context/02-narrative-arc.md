# 02 — Narrative arc

## The story in seven beats

1. **It happened to us.** We sold to one team at Zoom. We got 90,000+ calls.
2. **It happens because of how software is built.** Apps can connect one person or
   everyone. Not one team.
3. **It hurts both sides.** IT carries risk. Vendors carry data they don't want.
4. **It is everywhere, and growing.** Real breaches. More integrations every month.
   AI tools want even more data.
5. **Gatekeeper fixes it.** A gate in the middle. Only the right team's data passes.
6. **We can build it fast.** Filter after the fetch. No new connectors needed for the MVP.
7. **Here is where we start, here is what we don't know yet, here is the decision we need.**

Then, clearly separated: **side quests** (MCP and API access), and the **appendix**.

## One metaphor, used everywhere

**The building and the floor.**

- A company is a building with many floors.
- We were invited to visit **one floor** (the Workvivo team).
- Instead, we were given **the master key to the whole building**.
- Gatekeeper is the **front desk**: it checks who you are, and it only brings you
  documents from the floor you were invited to.

Use this metaphor on slides 2, 3, 7 and 8. Do not mix in other metaphors
(no water filters, no firewalls, no locks-and-vaults). One picture, repeated, is easier to
follow in a second language.

## Rules for writing for a non-technical, non-native English reader

- Max **12 words** per headline. Max **3 short lines** of body text per slide.
- Use the same word for the same thing every time:
  - "the company" (the data owner, e.g. Zoom)
  - "the vendor" (the requester, e.g. us)
  - "the IT team" (the company's admins and security people)
  - "the team" (the small group the vendor was sold to)
  - "Gatekeeper" (always this, never "the gate", "the proxy", "the layer" on slides)
- Prefer verbs over nouns: "Gatekeeper removes data" not "Gatekeeper performs data removal".
- Numbers as digits, large on screen: **90,000+**, **700+**.
- No sarcasm, no rhetorical questions on slides. Speaker can ask questions out loud.
- Every technical term on a slide gets a one-line plain definition on the same slide.

## Pacing

- Main deck: **15 slides**, about 15–20 minutes with discussion.
- Side quests: **2 slides**, clearly labeled.
- Appendix: as many as needed; only shown if Taka asks.
